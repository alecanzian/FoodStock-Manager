## Claim
Family members who shop for the household cannot reliably learn what is missing without asking someone else. When that communication fails, they rely on memory and either forget needed items or buy items the household already has.

### L01 - Who benefits, and what is excluded?
Both the people who shop and the people who need to be asked about "what’s missing?" benefit, because the solution implies a reliable way to know stock without needing communication, which makes the process more autonomous, with less forgetting and less wasteful buying of things already present.

### L02 - What boundary, delay or alternative mechanism matters?
- Alternatives: a paper list, a shared note, a group chat, or simply asking at home. Their weakness is that they depend on someone remembering to update them or to be asked.
- Boundary: Ease of access to the app, be it platform dependency or internet access.
- Delay: **TO DO**

### L03 - Who decides, bears cost or can block?
Each family member decides whether to scan or restock. 
The person who shops bears the cost of not knowing what is missing, and the person who is asked bears the cost of being interrupted.
Anyone can block the claim by not using the app, which makes adoption the main risk.

### L04 - What observation would weaken this claim?
If families already communicate what is missing reliably

### Next - Which decision would that evidence change?
- If the communication gap is real but the shared view is rarely consulted, the first increment should focus on a readable "what's missing" view that is easy to check before leaving.
- If the shared view is consulted but often wrong, the first increment should focus on making the stock values reliable: every add and subtract must be recorded correctly, including concurrency cases.
